What we actually measure

The four values, explained

Every statement in the assessment maps to one of these four areas. Here's what each one means, and why it matters.

Communication

The ability to express ideas clearly and to genuinely understand others, not just talking, but listening, reading a room, and adjusting your message to your audience.

Why it matters: most workplace conflict traces back to a miscommunication, not a disagreement about facts. Strong communicators prevent problems before they start.

Critical Thinking

The habit of questioning assumptions, weighing evidence, and considering more than one explanation before settling on a conclusion — rather than accepting the first plausible answer.

Why it matters: good critical thinkers catch flawed reasoning, their own included, before it turns into a costly decision.

Time Management

Prioritising what matters most, realistically estimating how long things take, and adapting a plan early when circumstances change, instead of reacting once a deadline is already at risk.

Why it matters: talent and effort only compound over time when they're pointed at the right priorities, in the right order.

Leadership

Taking initiative without being asked, staying composed under pressure, noticing what a team needs, and owning outcomes, good or bad, rather than deflecting responsibility.

Why it matters: leadership isn't a job title, it's a set of habits anyone can practice, starting with the smallest group project.
